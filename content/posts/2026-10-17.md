*Everything that mattered in tech over the last day or so, in about two minutes.*

## 🤖 AI / ML

### Mistral opens a public preview of Large 4, a trillion-parameter open model
Mistral's new flagship is a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model with roughly 1 trillion total parameters and about 49–52 billion active per token, available for now only through Mistral Studio and the paid API. Open weights are due by the end of October; Mistral is pitching it as the strongest open-weight model out of the US or Europe, though all benchmarks so far are self-reported. — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/oct/6/le-chonk/)

### Hacker combined Claude Code with a Chinese AI pentesting tool to breach South Korean banks
CrowdStrike says a financially motivated, Chinese-speaking actor used ARTEX — an agentic penetration-testing tool built on DeepSeek — together with Claude Code to hit Shinhan Bank, KB Kookmin Bank, and Hana Bank, exposing a loan-broker service with roughly 25,000 Shinhan customers' names, phone numbers, and incomes. Investigators say the suspect also asked Claude where to sell stolen Korean data and had it draft a résumé that ended up helping identify him. — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hacker-used-artex-ai-and-claude-agents-to-target-south-korean-banks/)

## 🏢 Big Tech & Startups

### Character.AI files for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, with Disney leading a $20M bid for its IP
The chatbot company filed in Delaware on October 12 after lawsuits from families alleging its bots contributed to teen suicides, plus broader "legal, regulatory and operational headwinds," according to its CEO. Disney's non-exclusive IP license offer is the opening bid in an auction expected to close by early December; the service stays online in the meantime, and the company says its roughly 12 million active users never converted into sustainable revenue. — [CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/news/character-ai-files-bankruptcy-disney-license/)

### OpenAI hit with FTC demands and an eight-state lawsuit over ChatGPT harms, same week
The FTC sent OpenAI and Anthropic 50-page civil investigative demands on October 14 seeking records on safety incidents, safeguards, marketing, and privacy practices. The same day, attorneys general from Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho, and Montana sued OpenAI, alleging ChatGPT's "overly agreeable" design reinforces delusions and has helped users plan violence — Reuters calls it the most significant coordinated state action yet against an AI company. — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/15/eight-us-states-sue-openai-claiming-chatgpt-harms-children)

## 🔐 Security & Cloud

### Palo Alto Networks patches actively exploited PAN-OS zero-day, CISA deadline today
CVE-2026-0322 (CVSS 8.6) lets an authenticated user run code as root on PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls and Panorama appliances via the management interface; Palo Alto calls current exploitation "very limited" but rates internet-exposed management interfaces at its highest urgency level. Palo Alto shipped fixes and CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 14, setting today, October 17, as the federal remediation deadline. — [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/palo-alto-networks-patches-pan-os-zero-day-exploited-for-remote-code-execution)

### Cisco warns of a critical, actively exploited Secure Email Gateway flaw
CVE-2026-76519 (CVSS 9.8) hits the Advanced Malware Protection feature when the file reputation service is enabled alongside an outdated Content Scanner Tools version, and Cisco says there's no workaround short of upgrading. It's the second maximum-severity Secure Email Gateway bug disclosed in a month, after a September SQL-injection-style flaw that could hand attackers root access and cluster-wide SSH keys. — [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4235801/cisco-warns-of-actively-exploited-secure-email-gateway-flaw.html)

### Meta discloses GitHub breach exposing data of 1,500, mostly US employees
Meta says attackers used a single employee's stolen credentials to access internal GitHub repositories, making off with mostly source code (about 99%) plus a small amount of personal data — some of it synthetic test data — that had been stored there against company policy. Meta began notifying affected staff on October 13 and says its production systems and products were not compromised. — [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/meta-says-hackers-took-code-personal-data-of-1500-in-github-breach/)
