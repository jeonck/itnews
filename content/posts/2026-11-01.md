*Everything that mattered in tech over the last day or so, in about two minutes.*

## 🤖 AI / ML

### Google's prototype data-center satellite comes online, writes a haiku from orbit
Google's Project Suncatcher satellite — built by Planet Labs, carrying four Google TPUs, and launched on a Falcon 9 in early October — finished commissioning and is now running Google DeepMind's Gemma 4 in space, with its first output being a haiku about orbiting Earth. It's an early but real test of Google's long-term bet on orbital data centers for AI inference, with two more satellites planned for 2027 if the hardware holds up against thermal stress and radiation. — [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/googles-first-data-center-satellite-is-online-and-writing-poetry/)

### OpenAI ships GPT-6.1 Terra at half the price, aimed at computer-use agents
OpenAI launched GPT-6.1 Terra on October 26 as a mid-tier model built for "computer use" — reading screens and operating applications — priced at $1/$5 per million input/output tokens, half the cost of GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol. OpenAI says it completes computer-use tasks over three times faster than GPT-6 Astra, positioning it as the cheap, fast option for agentic workflows in ChatGPT and Codex rather than the top-end reasoning tier. — [Source](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/openai-launches-gpt-61-terra-with-3x-faster-ai-agents-50-percent-lower-pricing/)

### Google unveils Gemini 4 Carbon, a cybersecurity-focused frontier model — but you can't use it yet
Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28 as its next frontier model, with access initially restricted to select cybersecurity defenders through its Fairwind Program before a wider staged rollout. Reporting is split on whether Carbon is a genuinely new model or an upgraded Gemini 4 Argon, but the cybersecurity-first access strategy is a notable departure from Google's usual broad launches. — [Source](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/google-launches-gemini-4-carbon-ai-model-with-cybersecurity-focus-2)

## 🛠 Development

### BoxLang AI 3.6 gives JVM agents browser control, voice, and a simpler MCP client
The JVM-based BoxLang AI framework shipped version 3.6 with browser automation via a new Playwright module, 26 audio tools, a streaming `aiSpeakStream()` function supported across OpenAI, ElevenLabs, Cartesia, Mistral and Gemini, and a stdio-based MCP client that launches MCP servers as child processes without needing an HTTP port. It also adds automatic context fencing to help guard against indirect prompt injection — a small but concrete sign of agent-security features becoming table stakes in agent frameworks. — [Source](https://daily.dev/posts/boxlang-ai-3-6-your-agent-now-has-eyes-ears-and-a-voice-kldfgxf8n)

## 🏢 Big Tech & Startups

### Guidewire to buy AI underwriting startup hyperexponential for up to $2.1 billion
Insurance software maker Guidewire agreed on October 27 to acquire London-based hyperexponential, an AI-native underwriting platform, for a $1.7 billion cash base plus up to $400 million more tied to post-closing revenue targets. Guidewire is funding the deal with a $1 billion term loan from Bank of America and Citibank, more than doubling its debt — a sign of how aggressively incumbents are paying up for "agentic underwriting" capability. — [Source](https://www.guidewire.com/ja/about/press-center/press-releases/20261027/guidewire-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-hyperexponential)

### Deepgram acquires Daily, the company behind the open-source Pipecat voice AI framework
Voice AI company Deepgram announced on October 29 that it's acquiring Daily, which built the widely used open-source Pipecat framework for real-time voice and video AI pipelines. Deepgram says Pipecat will stay open source and vendor-neutral, and is also launching an open protocol for voice agents co-developed with the Pipecat team — part of a broader consolidation wave in voice AI infrastructure alongside rivals like LiveKit and Vapi. — [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/deepgram-acquires-pipecat-maker-daily-to-bolster-its-voice-ai-infrastructure/)

### Meta posts its first negative free cash flow since going public, as AI capex climbs
Meta reported negative free cash flow of about $2.1 billion in Q3 — its first negative quarter since its 2012 IPO — after raising the low end of its 2026 capex guidance to $135 billion. Credit-default-swap pricing on Meta's debt hit record highs the same day, underscoring how the AI infrastructure buildout is now visibly straining even the best-funded tech balance sheets. — [Source](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/meta-reports-negative-free-cash-flow-for-first-time-since-2012)

## 🔐 Security & Cloud

### Critical Linux kernel TCP flaw (CVSS 9.8) still unpatched on RHEL and Ubuntu
CVE-2026-98432, a use-after-free in the Linux kernel's IPv4 TCP handshake code affecting version 4.4 and later, lets a remote unauthenticated peer potentially leak or corrupt kernel memory or crash the box — Red Hat rates it Critical and says it could lead to remote code execution, and Anthropic says its Claude model independently built a working root-access proof-of-concept against multipath-TCP listeners. Upstream and several distros patched it between October 11–18, but as of October 31 RHEL (8/9/10) and Ubuntu still have no fixed kernel available. — [Source](https://atlasum.net/blog/cve-2026-98432-linux-ipv4-tcp-uaf)

### Binance confirms record $6.3 billion hack; FBI blames North Korea, withdrawals resume
Binance disclosed the largest crypto theft on record — roughly $6.3 billion stolen on October 29 — which the FBI attributes to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, the same actors blamed for the $1.5 billion Bybit hack. Binance says it has covered roughly 90–95% of user losses from its reserves and has resumed withdrawals in phases, while about $3 billion of the stolen funds have reportedly been frozen. — [Source](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/31/binance-hack-asset-coverage-withdrawals.html)

### OpenAI patches ChatGPT bug that let a shared link hijack private conversations
Security firm Parameter found a chained flaw in ChatGPT's share-link feature that let a malicious shared chat run attacker code inside a victim's logged-in session — able to list and exfiltrate the victim's private conversations after a single click. OpenAI fixed the issue by October 26 and paid a $5,000 bounty; no exploitation in the wild was found, but it's a sharp reminder of how much attack surface chat-sharing and embedded previews can open up. — [Source](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/a-shared-chatgpt-link-could-run.html)
